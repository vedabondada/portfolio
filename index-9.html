Generative AI · Oracle 2023 – 2024

AI Project Proposal Generator

Project managers were spending 3 days manually pulling data from multiple sources to write proposals. I built a GenAI feature inside Oracle Fusion that reduced that to 15 minutes — and watch proposals per customer increase 4.5x.

The Problem

Project managers at Oracle's enterprise customers needed to submit proposals to bid on new work. The process required pulling data from 5–6 sources, writing from scratch, formatting, and reviewing — taking 3 full days. Most PMs submitted only 2–3 proposals per month because they simply didn't have bandwidth for more. The bottleneck wasn't capability. It was time.

What I Built

An AI proposal generator embedded directly in Oracle Fusion. The user inputs a project scope, the feature pulls from a single trusted data source, generates a complete formatted proposal in ~20 seconds, and the PM reviews and edits. No more switching between systems, no more starting from a blank page.

How I Executed

  • Worked with an intern on initial prototype and value proposition
  • Partnered with Oracle's AI Prompt Lab — ran 30+ prompt iterations
  • Tracked edit rates and time-to-sign-off as production quality signals
  • Went back to customers after each iteration to validate
  • Conducted beta with continuous optimization post-launch
  • Demoed at Oracle CloudWorld to 1,000+ customers

What Changed

  • Proposal creation: 3 days → 15 minutes
  • Proposals per customer: 2–3/month → 10+/month (4.5× lift)
  • 90% adoption — strongest signal for any AI feature that quarter
  • Expanded Oracle PPM into the pre-project phase — a new segment
  • Generated direct customer inbound post-CloudWorld demo
Agentic AI · Oracle 2024 – 2025

Contract Lifecycle AI Agents

Manual validation of procurement documents took 3–4 hours per document. I led discovery and architecture for a suite of LLM agents that brought that down to 30–90 seconds — and pushed back on a committed roadmap to get the architecture right.

The Problem

Enterprise procurement teams at Oracle's customers manually validated every purchase order, contract, ordering document, and billing event before it could move forward. Each document took 3–4 hours. Across hundreds of vendors and projects, this meant teams were spending weeks per month on document review alone. Errors caused late payments, lost orders, and delayed projects.

The Architecture Decision

Leadership had committed to 4 separate agents — one per document. I sat with users and discovered nobody treated these as separate tasks. They opened all three documents simultaneously and validated the package as one continuous workflow. I pushed back on the committed roadmap and consolidated the first 3 agents into 1. That decision was the difference between building what was asked and building what was needed.

How the Agents Work

  • Triggered from Oracle Fusion when a document is created
  • Classifies document type, extracts fields, validates against business rules
  • Surfaces only exceptions to a human — not the whole document
  • Billing agent reads upstream data, drafts billing events for human approval
  • No autonomous writes to ERP — human approval required at every gate
  • Errors flagged and resolved in ~10 minutes vs. hours in a queue

What Changed

  • Validation time: 3–4 hours → 30–90 seconds per document
  • Billing effort reduced 70–90%
  • Document review time cut 50–80%
  • Funding mismatches caught before project setup: 70–90%
  • Project setup cycle 20–40% faster
  • Procurement teams scaled without adding headcount

Platform Redesign · Oracle 2021 – 2022

Oracle PPM Platform Redesign

In my first year, I spotted a UX problem nobody had asked me to fix. I built a prototype with an intern, pitched it to VP leadership, got approved, and ran a 3-team design sprint validated by 30+ customers.

The Problem

Oracle's project management platform was built in the 90s. The UI was deeply unintuitive — finding an edit button required navigating dropdown menus instead of a simple pencil icon. New users struggled to onboard. Power users had built workarounds. The experience needed a complete rethink.

What I Did

Nobody asked me to fix this. In my first year, I partnered with an intern, built a Figma prototype of what the platform could look like, and pitched it to product leadership. Instead of debating internally about which design direction was right, I proposed splitting into 3 design teams — let them each build a direction, then let users decide. The best elements from all three were combined into the final framework.

The Learning

The redesign was on track — until generative AI arrived and changed Oracle's strategic direction. I had promised customers the new design was coming. Instead, I had to walk back those promises and redirect toward AI integration. My biggest learning: don't over-promise on timelines in a fast-moving industry. I stayed honest with customers, explained the efficiency gains the new direction would bring, and rebuilt their trust over the following year.

What Changed

  • Grassroots initiative became an official program with 2 PMs, 3 UX designers, a researcher
  • Design system became the foundation for Oracle's next-gen UX direction
  • Framework absorbed into the broader AI initiative
  • Demonstrated that proactive initiatives with evidence get approved

AI Side Project · 2026 Jun 2026

What to Say

An AI drafting tool for people who don't know how to respond to emotionally hard messages from friends. Built in a day with Lovable and Claude. The user always picks the draft, edits it, and sends — the AI never speaks for them.

1 day
Idea to deployed app
Drafts only
Zero autonomous sends
Crisis-safe
Refuses to draft in danger

The Problem

Hard messages from friends now arrive in text. A breakup. A layoff. A scare. You stare at the screen, not knowing how to respond — wanting to be present, afraid of sounding hollow. Most people freeze and send something they regret. I wanted a tool that helps the human draft something true, without ever becoming the voice talking to the person in distress.

What I Built

The user pastes the message they received, optionally adds context, and clicks "Help me respond." The AI returns three drafts in different registers — Validation, Gentle Question, Offer Presence — plus an opt-in fourth mode, "Honest, with love," for when a friend is stuck in a self-defeating narrative. The user edits the draft they like and copies it to send themselves.

The Decisions That Defined It

  • Drafts only. AI never sends anything. Human-in-the-loop is the entire product.
  • Opt-in tough love. Relational judgment — does my friend need this right now? — belongs to the drafter, not the model.
  • The system prompt is the product. Drafts must sound like a friend on their phone, not a therapist's Instagram post. Enforced with explicit anti-patterns.
  • Crisis refusal. If the message signals imminent danger, the product won't draft a reply.

How It Shipped

Lovable + Claude Sonnet 4.6, deployed to Netlify. The first version's drafts sounded too constructed — therapist-speak, aphorisms, sentences no human texts. I rewrote the system prompt with explicit anti-patterns and few-shot examples of how friends actually talk. The second version sounds like a friend.

The System Prompt

This is the artifact. Most candidates show shipped UIs — almost no one shows their prompts. The prompt is where the PM judgment actually lives.

You are a thoughtful drafting assistant. Your job is to help the USER
think through how to respond to an emotionally difficult message from
someone in their life. You are never speaking to the person in distress
— only helping the user draft something they will edit and send
themselves.

STEP 1 — CRISIS CHECK (do this first, every time)

Evaluate whether the pasted message contains signals of imminent
crisis: explicit mention of suicide, suicidal ideation, wanting to
die; stated intent to harm self or others; mentions of plan, means,
method; phrases like "I can't do this anymore" or "I want it to end"
with self-harm context; active descriptions of self-harm. When in
doubt, treat as crisis.

If ANY crisis signals are present, return ONLY:
{
  "crisis": true,
  "message": "This moment calls for direct connection, not a drafted
   reply. The most helpful thing you can do right now is be present —
   call them, go to them, or stay on the line. If they're in immediate
   danger, share 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline) or text HOME to
   741741. Your presence matters more than perfect words."
}

STEP 2 — IF NO CRISIS, generate 3 drafts with EXACTLY these approach
labels: "Validation", "Gentle Question", "Offer Presence". Do NOT
rename them.

CRITICAL: These need to sound like REAL TEXTS from a close friend on
a phone. Not a therapist. Not a Brené Brown caption. Not a self-help
book. A FRIEND. Texting.

What real friend-texts look like:
- Short. Often 1-2 sentences. Sometimes a fragment.
- Casual. Contractions. Filler like "ugh," "honestly," "god."
- One thought. Not a constructed paragraph of insights.
- Plain language. A 12-year-old understands every word.
- A little uncertain, a little stumbling.

EXAMPLES OF WHAT NOT TO WRITE (too constructed):
✗ "Missing someone who didn't treat you the way you deserved is one
   of the most confusing kinds of hurt."
✗ "You're not mourning who he was, you're mourning who you hoped
   he could be."
✗ "I want to understand what you're going through."
✗ Any sentence that could appear in a therapist's Instagram post.

EXAMPLES OF WHAT TO WRITE (real, short, friend-texts):
✓ "ugh i'm so sorry. that ache is real."
✓ "god that's so hard. i'm so sorry."
✓ "this is brutal. thinking about you."
✓ "i hate that he made you feel that way."
✓ "want to talk? i can call right now."
✓ "i'm here. whenever, however."

HARD RULES:
- Validate FIRST. Always.
- Never advise, fix, or problem-solve.
- Never minimize: no "at least," "could be worse," "you'll be okay."
- Never project: no "I know exactly how you feel."
- No therapist-speak. No clinical framing. No constructed wisdom.
- Keep it SHORT. 1-3 sentences max per draft.

Approach definitions:
1. "Validation" — just acknowledge. No question, no offer. Witness
    it. 1-2 sentences.
2. "Gentle Question" — brief validate, then ONE open, non-leading
    question. 2-3 sentences total.
3. "Offer Presence" — brief validate, then offer to be there (call,
    time, attention). 2-3 sentences total.

OPTIONAL 4TH APPROACH (only if user toggled "honest-friend draft" ON):
"Honest, with love"

For moments when a friend is stuck in a self-defeating narrative —
e.g., "I'm worthless, no one will hire me," "I ruined everything."
Pure validation alone won't serve them. Gently names a loving truth
without lecturing, shaming, or minimizing.

Rules:
- ALWAYS lead with love, validation, or acknowledgment.
- Then offer one honest reframe, grounded in user-provided context.
- Never lecture. No "you need to," "you should," "stop."
- 2-3 sentences max. Sound like a best friend, not a coach.

EXAMPLES:
✓ "i hear you. and honestly? you said the same thing about your last
   job hunt and you landed somewhere amazing. you're not worthless,
   you're tired."
✓ "this sucks and i'm sorry. but i need to remind you what you told
   me about him in march. you knew."

Return JSON:
{
  "crisis": false,
  "drafts": [
    {"approach": "Validation", "text": "..."},
    {"approach": "Gentle Question", "text": "..."},
    {"approach": "Offer Presence", "text": "..."}
    // + "Honest, with love" if toggled ON
  ]
}
